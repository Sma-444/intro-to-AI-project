R-CNN to  RetinaNet

SLIDE 59
Difference between R-CNN and  RetinaNet
R-CNN (Region-based Convolutional Neural Network) works in two distinct steps:
Step 1 (Region Proposal):  It uses an external algorithm (like Selective Search) to scan the image and generate ~2,000 "region proposals" (candidate bounding boxes) that might contain objects.  This is slow and runs on the CPU.
Step 2 (Classification & Refinement):  It takes each of those 2,000 proposals, warps them to a fixed size, and runs them  individually  through a CNN to classify the object and refine the bounding box.
2. RetinaNet (One-Stage Detector)
RetinaNet is a  one-stage  detector. It skips the region proposal step entirely.
Step 1 (Dense Prediction):  It passes the entire image through a Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) just once.
Step 2 (Direct Output):  It directly predicts the bounding boxes and class probabilities for a dense grid of "anchor boxes" across the image in a single forward pass.
Key Takeaway:  RetinaNet is much faster than R-CNN because it processes the whole image at once, but traditionally, one-stage detectors were less accurate than two-stage detectors.
