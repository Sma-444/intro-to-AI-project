Type 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) .
How it works: The pre-trained GPT-3 model is further trained on a smaller, high-quality dataset of labeled examples. For instance, to create a coding assistant like Codex, you would fine-tune GPT-3 on millions of pairs of (problem description, solution code).
Type 2: In-Context Learning (Few-Shot, One-Shot, Zero-Shot)
This is a unique capability of large models like GPT-3.  It doesn't involve training at all  . The model learns to perform a task from the instructions and examples provided directly in the prompt.
How it works : You show the model a few examples of the task you want it to do directly in your input text. The model recognizes the pattern and applies it to a new, final example .
Few-Shot : Providing a few examples in the prompt.
One-Shot : Providing just one example.
Zero-Shot : Providing only an instruction, no examples.

Path 3: Training from Human Feedback (RLHF).
This is the advanced technique used to create models like  ChatGPT , making them more helpful, harmless, and honest.
Step 1 (Supervised Fine-Tuning): First, human labelers write example conversations to teach the model the desired tone and format.
Step 2 (Training a Reward Model - RM): The model generates multiple responses to a prompt. Human labelers rank these responses from best to worst. This data is used to train a separate "Reward Model" that learns to score responses based on human preferences .
Step 3 (Reinforcement Learning): Finally, GPT-3 is  fine-tuned  using a reinforcement learning algorithm (like PPO). The model gets a "reward" score from the Reward Model for its output and learns to adjust its responses to maximize that reward, effectively aligning its behavior with human values .

Evolution of GPT Models
GPT models evolved from text generation systems into multimodal, reasoning-capable, and increasingly agentic AI systems.
Stage 1: Foundation
GPT-1 (2018):  Introduced pretrained decoder-only Transformer for NLP tasks
GPT-2 (2019):  Scaled up text generation and improved generative quality.
GPT-3 (2020):  Demonstrated strong few-shot prompting ability at large scale.
Stage 2: Conversational & Multimodal
GPT-3.5 (2022):  Improved instruction following and conversation.
GPT-4 (2023):  Significantly stronger reasoning and multimodal understanding.
GPT-4o (2024):  Enabled real-time multimodal interaction with text, image, and audio.
GPT-4.5 (2025):  Improved conversational quality and intent understanding.
Stage 3: Reasoning & Agents
GPT-5 (2025):  Unified fast response and deeper reasoning.
GPT-5.5 (2026):  Improved coding, research, and agentic workflows.
GPT-5.6 (2026):  Further improved reasoning efficiency and technical performance.
GPT-6 (2026):  Latest generation with further advances in general AI capability.

A prefix to tell the model what to do with the input text.
T5 treats different NLP tasks as text-to-text problems, using task prefixes to specify what the model should do.
T5 uses a unified text-to-text framework, allowing one appropriately trained model to perform multiple NLP tasks, such as translation, summarization, and classification, guided by task prefixes.
The key takeaway: T5's innovation is not that task prefixes magically enable new abilities, but that different NLP tasks can share the same model architecture and text-generation interface.
